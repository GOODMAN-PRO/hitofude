# HITOFUDE — *One Stroke Short*

一筆 / HITOFUDE
Original manga series bible. Season 1.

---

## LOGLINE

At an academy where the whole city can see your rank floating beside your shoulder, the untouchable Rank 9 who nobody is allowed to speak to first is secretly a fussy seventeen-year-old running his dead grandmother's pickle shop in a cat-print apron. The girl at the very bottom of the seating chart — sunny, apologetic, permanently invisible — is secretly foul-mouthed, ruthless, and the best blade hand in the prefecture, and at five in the morning their delivery routes cross.

---

## TONE MANIFESTO — the ten rules

1. **The quiet is the meal.** Fights are seasoning. If a chapter's best page is a fight page, that chapter failed.
2. **Talky and dense.** Short bubbles, many of them. Two people in a small room saying eleven things is the house style.
3. **Nobody explains their feelings.** They explain the rice bran. The feeling is underneath the rice bran.
4. **Comedy first, then the knife.** A gag runs for three panels and the fourth panel is quietly devastating. Never the reverse.
5. **The System is never a villain.** It is weather. It is correct and it does not understand. The horror is bureaucratic, not evil.
6. **Cruelty here is polite.** Nobody bullies. They are *considerate* at you. They smile and route around you. That is worse and we know it.
7. **Both leads are hiding, and neither is hiding something cool.** He hides an apron. She hides a temper. Embarrassment, not tragedy.
8. **Domestic beat every chapter, no exceptions.** Food, laundry, a bandage, a bus ride, dishes. If you cannot find one, the chapter is not written yet.
9. **Wit over melodrama.** No speeches. Anyone who starts a speech gets interrupted by a kettle.
10. **Sayo never gets magic.** Not in a burst of emotion, not at the climax, not ever. That promise is the spine of the series.

## THE WORLD

### The city — NAGIHAMA (凪浜)

A mid-sized river city, population around four hundred thousand. A wide slow river splits it; a long green bridge crosses it; the hills on the north bank are full of narrow stepped streets and vending machines that hum.

The heart of the story is **Sakaechō Arcade** — a covered shopping street, eleven minutes end to end, roof panels yellowed, a leak near the fish stall that nobody will fix. Two shops matter:

- **FUJINAMI TSUKEMONO** — a pickle shop, four generations, three tatami of retail floor and a back room that is entirely ceramic crocks. Run by a seventeen-year-old since last spring.
- **KIKUCHI HAMONO** — knives, sharpening, repairs. Hand-painted sign in the window: **NO WINDOWS AT THIS COUNTER.**

Between them: seven hundred metres, a bus stop, a konbini with a broken hot-drinks cabinet, and a set of concrete river steps that are the single best place in Nagihama to eat something at 5:40 in the morning.

### The magic — SIGNING

You draw in the air with two fingers. The air keeps the line.

That's it. That's the system.

A **stroke** is one continuous line hanging in the air where you drew it. Strokes stay while you hold them. Close a shape with your strokes and the shape **seals** — it discharges, and the effect matches the shape. Simple shapes, simple effects. Complex shapes, real force.

Your **Stroke Count** is how many open strokes you can hold at once before they collapse. Stroke Count is your rank. One to nine.

**Rank 1** holds one stroke. One line seals nothing — a line is not a shape. You get a spark, a warm patch, a nudge. Enough to light a cigarette. Not enough to be a person, socially.
**Rank 9** holds nine. Nine strokes is a sigil with interior structure, layered seals, a shape inside a shape. Nine is a weather event with a name.

**How to explain it in three panels:**
1. Two fingers move; a black brushstroke hangs in the air behind them.
2. Three more strokes; the four lines close into a square.
3. The square flips to white and shatters outward.

**Cost.** Held strokes numb your hands — fingers first, then to the wrist, cold and clumsy. Overdraw and your line quality goes: the stroke comes out shaky, forked, wet at the ends. A bad hand day looks like bad handwriting. Nosebleeds are for real overdraw and are considered vulgar.

**Hand.** Every signer's stroke has a personality — thickness, speed, the wobble at the turn, how wet the terminals are. Experts recognise each other's *hand* the way you recognise handwriting. Fujinami Kaoru's hand is famously clean, even, and boring. Kikuchi Sayo's single stroke was once described by a proctor as "the most beautiful single stroke I have ever seen in twenty years." Nobody cared. One stroke does nothing.

**How it draws in B&W:**
- Open stroke: a **solid black brush line**, tapered start, wet blunt terminal. Real ink weight. It hangs.
- Sealing: the black **inverts to white** with a thin screentone halo bleeding off the edges.
- Discharge: the white shape **shatters into radial hatch lines**. No glow, no gradients. Hatch.
- Failed cast: strokes **sag, drip, and break into small round dots** that fall and vanish. Comic and sad.
- Numb hands: draw the hand in **flat 20% tone** with no interior linework, like it went out of focus.
- Nine strokes at once: a page-wide tangle of black brushwork with white cutting through it. Expensive. Use it four times a season.

### The academy — KUDAN ACADEMY (九段学院)

*Kudan* — "nine ranks." A municipal academy of applied signing. Ages 15–18, three years, eleven hundred students.

**The school is the Tower.** Nine floors, one rank each. Your ID admits you to your floor and every floor below it. That is the entire architecture of the institution and of the social order.

| Floor | What's up there | Who gets in |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Homerooms, the annex (bike shelter with tables), lockers | everyone |
| 2 | Second-year homerooms, the small library | Rank 2+ |
| 3 | **The cafeteria** | Rank 3+ |
| 4 | Practice halls, showers, the good vending machine | Rank 4+ |
| 5 | Faculty, the infirmary that has actual supplies | Rank 5+ |
| 6 | The good library, quiet rooms, the courtyard-over-the-court | Rank 6+ |
| 7 | Proctor offices, the archive | Rank 7+ |
| 8 | The upper hall | Rank 8+ |
| 9 | The roof | Rank 9 |

"Climbing the Tower" is stairs. It is completely literal and completely mundane and that is the joke and the wound. The elevator has nine buttons; the ones above your rank are greyed out in your own eyes only, so you watch a stranger press 6 and the button lights for them and stays dark for you.

An **Ascent** is a proctored floor-clearing exercise, four or five times a year, run in the practice halls of 6 through 9. Ascents are where the season's actual fights happen. There are two.

## THE SYSTEM LAYER — the TALLY

Rolled out twenty-two years ago by a ministry that no longer exists under that name. Nobody knows how it works. People asked for about six years and then stopped, the way you stop asking about weather.

It is not a game. There is no menu, no inventory, no quest log. It is a public utility that renders a small window near a person's **shoulder** — never overhead, which everyone agrees would be tacky.

```
┌──────────────────────────┐
│ KIKUCHI, SAYO            │
│ RANK 1  ·  SEAT 41 / 41  │
└──────────────────────────┘
```

**What it shows:** family name, given name, rank, and your seat number in whatever ranked room you're standing in. A **LEDGER** line flashes for one second when anything is added or subtracted.

**When windows appear:**
- When you look directly at a person for more than about two seconds.
- When you enter a ranked space (classroom, hall, Tower floor, some businesses).
- When something measurable happens: a cast, a test, a floor entry, a result.

**You cannot turn it off.** You can look away. Looking away deliberately is rude and everyone can tell.

**What being measured means socially:** nobody ever has to guess who you are. So nobody ever bothers to ask.

**The System's one great flaw:** it is never wrong and it never understands. It reports what happened in the terms it has. Its verdicts are technically correct and emotionally catastrophic:

```
┌──────────────────────────┐
│ NO MAGICAL ACTIVITY      │
│ DETECTED                 │
│ RESULT: NULL             │
└──────────────────────────┘
```

**The Turn.** Last Monday of every month, 06:00. Citywide recalculation. Some people set alarms. Some people go and stand outside so it happens where their family can't see. The window says `ADJUSTMENT: +1`, or `-1`, or, most often, `ADJUSTMENT: 0`.

**PENDING.** Children get no window until their tenth birthday, when it activates at 06:00 with a chime. Until then their window reads `PENDING`, which children find exciting and adults find unbearable.

**NO DATA.** Anyone who was over fifty at rollout was never measured. Their window is a blank frame with a dash. They are called the Unmeasured, and depending on who's talking it means *irrelevant* or *free*.

**UNLISTED.** A student can fall off the roll entirely — Rank 0. The System renders no window at all for them. Not a blank window. Nothing. People's eyes genuinely slide off. It is the most frightening thing in this series and there are no fights in it.

## THE SOCIAL COST — daily-life mechanics

These are not metaphors. Write them as literal blocking.

**1. Seating is rank order.** Descending, front-left to back-right. Seat 1 is by the window at the front. Seat 41 is the back corner by the recycling bin. When your rank moves, you move your desk. In front of everybody. Monday morning, first period: **the Shuffle**. Kaoru has sat in Seat 1 for fourteen months. Sayo has sat in Seat 41 since she arrived.

**2. Speaking order is posted.** In homeroom, group work, any club: the Tally renders a **SPEAKING QUEUE** on the wall, rank descending. You may speak out of turn. Nothing happens. Your words simply do not get a window, and people's eyes drift to whoever's window is lit. Being ignored here is not malice; it is an optical effect.

**3. Floors.** See the table. Ranks 1 and 2 cannot reach the cafeteria on 3. They eat in the annex — a covered bicycle shelter with four folding tables. In February this is genuinely cold. In June the roof leaks in exactly one place and everyone knows which table it is.

**4. Greet down, never up.** Custom, not rule, which makes it unbreakable. A Rank 2 does not say good morning to a Rank 7 unless spoken to first. This means **nobody is permitted to speak to a Rank 9 first, ever.** Kaoru did not choose to be cold. He stopped starting conversations that only ever went in one direction. Everyone reads the result as arrogance. Fixing it by being warm is impossible: warmth from Rank 9 reads as a test or a threat.

**5. Group Assessment averages the group's rank.** So a project group with Sayo in it scores lower before anyone does any work. Nobody is cruel about this. They are *kind* about it. "Ah, Kikuchi-san, you must be so busy—" The politeness is the cruelty. Write it that way every time.

**6. Address by rank, not by age.** School register: Ranks 7–9 take the suffix **-shi** (氏), a cold bureaucratic honorific used in documents. "Fujinami-shi." It is technically respectful and functionally a wall. When somebody says *Fujinami-kun* to his face it is, in this series, a bigger event than any fight. (See style.md for the full honorific ladder — it is the romance's progress bar.)

**7. Rank leaks into the city.** Bus boarding, konbini queues, hospital triage in some wards, the ramen counter on the north bank. Places that refuse to honour it are treasured and slightly outlaw. Hence the sign in the Kikuchi window, and the fact that at 5 a.m. in Sakaechō Arcade there is nobody around to look at anybody, and the windows might as well not exist.

That is where the series takes place. Not the Tower. The arcade, at five in the morning, when nobody is looking.
